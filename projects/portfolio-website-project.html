My Personal Portfolio

Responsive Website UI/UX Design

01. Project Overview

As a self-taught designer, I created this portfolio website as a space where potential collaborators and recruiters can easily explore my work across UI, UX, web and graphic design. My goal was to craft a platform that reflects my design aesthetics, technical curiosity and commitment to continuous growth.

Building the site from scratch allowed me to own every stage of the process, from designing in Figma to developing, deploying and refining a live website. It also gave me the opportunity to gain hands-on experience with GitHub Pages, Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity to better understand how visitors interact with my work.

  • Role: UI Designer & Front-End Developer
  • Scope: Responsive Website Design and Development
  • Tools: Figma, VS Code, HTML, CSS, Javascript, Github Pages, Google Analytics, Microsoft Clarity

02. The Challenge



Many portfolio templates provide a fast way to publish work, but I wanted my portfolio to communicate more than just finished projects. It needed to demonstrate my ability to design interfaces, establish a design system and translate those designs into a responsive website through front-end development.

To achieve that, I set a few goals and accepted several challenges along the way.

    Goals:

  • • Design and develop a portfolio entirely from scratch.
  • • Showcase both my design process and front-end implementation.
  • • Create a clean, easy-to-scan experience that keeps the focus on the work.
  • • Build a foundation that can grow alongside my skills and future projects.

    Key Constraints:

  • • Maintaining visual consistency and responsiveness across different screen sizes.
  • • Learning and applying front-end techniques while actively building the project.
  • • Optimizing high-quality imagery without sacrificing loading performance.
  • • Writing concise case studies that clearly communicate my design thinking.

03. Design Direction

Before designing the interface, I reviewed portfolios from experienced designers across UI/UX, branding, graphic design and motion design. Rather than focusing on visual style alone, I looked for common patterns in how they presented their work, structured their case studies and guided visitors through their projects. A few principles consistently stood out and became the foundation for my own portfolio.

    Key Constraints:

  • • Maintaining visual consistency and responsiveness across different screen sizes.
  • • Learning and applying front-end techniques while actively building the project.
  • • Optimizing high-quality imagery without sacrificing loading performance.
  • • Writing concise case studies that clearly communicate my design thinking.





Just as important were the decisions I chose not to make. I intentionally avoided decorative gradients, excessive visual effects and overly complex layouts that added little functional value. Every design element needed to support the content, reduce cognitive load and help visitors focus on the work itself.

04. Design Process

With the design direction established, I created a small design system before designing individual pages. Defining typography, spacing, colors and reusable components early gave the project a consistent visual language while making the transition from Figma to front-end development more efficient. Every design decision that followed was guided by a single objective: make the portfolio easy to navigate, quick to scan and focused on the work.




I designed the homepage around a minimal introduction that briefly presents who I am before directing attention to my selected projects. Keeping the work just one scroll away allows visitors to quickly understand what I do without navigating through multiple sections. Each project card pairs a large visual preview with concise supporting copy, giving recruiters the flexibility to either scan the highlights or dive deeper into the full case study.



I selected the Inter typeface for its clean, modern appearance and excellent readability across different screen sizes. A dark interface helps the UI mockups stand out while creating a more focused viewing experience, but I deliberately avoided pure black and pure white to maintain comfortable contrast during longer reading sessions. Together, these decisions reinforce the same philosophy established at the start of the project: remove unnecessary distractions, reduce cognitive load and let the work speak for itself.

06. The Final Experience




The completed portfolio brings together the principles established throughout the project: a clear visual hierarchy, restrained interface and responsive layouts that keep the work at the center of the experience. Every page was designed to help recruiters and collaborators quickly understand my projects while demonstrating my ability to carry a design from concept to a fully functional website.

07. Reflection


Building this portfolio reinforced that thoughtful preparation makes implementation significantly more efficient. Establishing a small design system before writing code gave the project a consistent visual language, simplified development and made future updates easier as the site continues to evolve.

If I continue developing this portfolio, I'd like to expand its accessibility, refine the case studies as I gain more professional experience, and continue improving the front-end implementation as my JavaScript skills grow. Like any design project, I see this portfolio as something that will evolve alongside my career rather than remain a finished product.
